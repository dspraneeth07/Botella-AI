## 1. What this sheet is for

Sheets 04–17 made the numbers defensible. Almost none of them are **legible**.

`own_weight: 0.31` is a fact about our shrinkage estimator, not information
about a shop. A bare `24` is arithmetic with the care removed. A raw confidence
of 0.95 is a claim the engine has not earned.

This sheet is where the pipeline learns to speak.

---

## 2. Three bugs found on the way in

### The card displayed the RAW confidence

```js
const conf = r.confidence != null ? (r.confidence >= 0.75 ? ... ) : { seg: 3, word: 'Good' };
const confPct = Math.round((r.confidence != null ? r.confidence : 0.6) * 100);
```

Three faults in two lines:

1. **`r.confidence` is the model's *stated* confidence.** Sheet 08 measured it
   at a mean of **0.48 against an actual hit rate of 0.23** — a coin flip with
   a decimal point, shown under the word that makes people trust it.
2. **`confPct` is a bare percentage.** "74%" invites a precision nobody has
   earned; the calibrated estimate's honest resolution is a band.
3. **An absent confidence defaulted to `{ word: 'Good' }` and 60%** — inventing
   a confidence claim for a card that made none. Same bug class as
   `Number(null) === 0`: an unknown rendered as a confident value.

Now: the calibrated field only, words only, and a card with no calibrated
confidence **claims none at all**. The sort tiebreak was reading the raw value
too, and now reads the calibrated one — ordering by how confident the engine
*sounds* rather than how often it is right is what sheet 13 exists to stop.

## 3. The provenance line is not optional

Every card whose `own_weight < 1` says where its numbers came from, **on the
card face, not behind the caret**:

> *Based on 14 similar stores in Texas — you have 3 weeks of your own history.*

A card whose numbers are partly borrowed must say so **where it is read**, not
where it is investigated.

**Generated by the pipeline, rendered here.** Sheet 15 builds the line and
sheet 16 verifies its numerals against the validated record, so the UI prefers
*that* string. It falls back to composing one from the same `Provenance` object
only when nothing arrived — because a missing sentence must never become a
missing disclosure in front of an owner.

**A cohort-weighted card arriving without one is a pipeline bug**, and is
surfaced as one on internal builds (`IS_INTERNAL`, i.e. QA/staging — never a
store build). Not a styling problem, and not something to paper over.

### The rung is not the place

`cohort_level` is one of `store | state | region | national` — the rung of the
ladder that answered. It is **not a place name**; the place arrives separately
as `cohort_region`. Printing the rung would produce *"14 similar stores in
state"*, which is precisely the bug sheet 15 hit from the other side. The
national rung names no place even when one is attached: it covers everywhere.

## 4. The evidence state is spoken, not scored

> *You're an emerging store — I'm using your data where I can and similar shops
> where I can't.*

Said **once at the top**, not repeated on every card, because it is a statement
about the *shop* rather than about any one recommendation. An owner can agree
with that sentence or argue with it. `own_weight 0.31` is neither.

An unknown state says **nothing** rather than guessing.

---

## 5. Constraint reasons make the care visible

> **Suggested order:** 24 units (2 cases of 12)
> *Adjusted: rounded down to 2 cases of 12*

An owner who sees a bare `24` wonders why not 25. The reason is the interesting
part, and showing it is what makes sheet 11 read as care rather than as
unexplained arithmetic.

Two rules keep it honest:

- **A quantity that is not a whole number of cases says only the units.**
  Claiming "2.08 cases" would be arithmetic nobody asked for.
- **The note only appears when the quantity was actually changed.** A note
  about a constraint that did not bind is noise, and noise is what teaches
  people to stop reading the notes that matter.

## 6. Two surfaces, one gate — and the check is server-side

> *"A client-side filter is not a boundary."*

It is not, and the reason is concrete: the payload still crossed the network,
so anybody with devtools open has the shadow cards and the p-values behind
them.

| | store surface | internal surface |
|---|---|---|
| route | `GET /api/recommendations/latest` | `GET /api/recommendations/internal/latest` |
| credential | the store's JWT | `X-Internal-Key`, a separate shared secret |
| shadow cards | subject to the gate | always visible |
| p-values, null means, gate reasoning | **never** | yes |

**A separate endpoint behind a separate credential** — not a query parameter
the browser can flip.

### What internal-only actually means today

Sheet 17 left gate *enforcement* off during the first quarter, so a store
currently still **sees** every card. It has never seen a p-value, and will not:
the field stripping is unconditional and does not depend on the rollout flag.
Those are two different questions and only one of them is waiting on evidence.

The store is told **that** some advice is still being measured, never **which**
or **how much**: the gate block on the store surface carries a single boolean.
*"Why am I seeing fewer cards"* has an answer; *"here is our p-value"* is not
that answer.

### The credential fails closed

With **no key configured the internal surface does not exist** — it 404s rather
than falling open, because a debugging endpoint that is accidentally public is
worse than one that is accidentally missing. A wrong key gets **404, not 403**:
an endpoint that answers *"wrong key"* confirms it exists. The comparison is
constant-time over the key.

> **An honest limitation.** There is no role on the JWT today, so "internal
> staff" is a shared secret rather than an identity. A proper role model is the
> right answer; inventing one inside a recommendation route is how a codebase
> ends up with two of them.

## 7. Raised, not changed: `MASK_CUSTOMER_IDENTITY`

The flag is `true` and **has not been touched**. What changed is that it now
has a **deadline**, and the deadline is not "when somebody notices".

The gate moves a family from shadow to live **automatically**, once it beats a
volume-matched null. `win_back` is one of those families. So the day this
becomes a live product problem is a day chosen by a p-value, not by a release
plan — and nobody will be watching for it.

The failure is quiet and complete: the gate proves win-back works, the cards go
live, and every one names a customer the owner **cannot identify**. The
recommendation is correct, measured, validated by sheets 14 and 16 — and
unusable. **None of those gates can catch it, because none of them asks whether
the owner can act on a correct card.**

Needs a product and privacy owner:

- Does the store see its own customers' names? (They are the store's customers
  and the store's data, which is an argument for yes.)
- If yes, under what consent — and does the demo path stay masked?
- If no, `win_back` should be **removed from the family list** rather than
  shipped in a form that cannot be acted on.

## 10. Not done

- **The fields render nothing until the pipeline supplies them.** `provenance`,
  `calibrated_confidence`, `constraint_reasons` and `evidence_state` are not
  yet on the `/latest` payload, because sheets 10–17 are not in the live path.
  That is the same migration dependency every sheet since 10 has named, and the
  UI degrades correctly: every function returns `null` when it has nothing
  honest to say, and the card renders nothing rather than a placeholder.
- **There is no internal *screen*.** The internal *surface* exists and is
  tested; what consumes it is a curl command today. A shadow-card review page
  is worth building once the gate has verdicts to review — which needs sheet 19.
- **`useCellTitles` was not needed here.** The sheet names it as the convention
  for truncated table cells; the recommendation surface is cards, not a table,
  and nothing new here clips. The convention is followed where it applies, not
  imported to look compliant.
- **`MASK_CUSTOMER_IDENTITY` is unresolved by design** — see §7. It needs a
  decision, not a commit.

## 11. The one-line summary

The pipeline learned to speak: every borrowed number says whose it is, every
constrained quantity says why, the evidence state became a sentence an owner
can argue with, and the confidence on the card is the calibrated one — while
the shadow cards and the statistics behind them moved to a surface the store's
browser cannot reach.

hard null = the same measurement on randomly chosen products that were equally primed to revert. It is deliberately harder than a plain random baseline, because the engine picks products whose recent sales have dipped and the metric asks whether they bounce — so part of any edge is regression to the mean, free to anyone. The edge column is the number that survives scrutiny.

What this says

  • 5 of 5 stores clear the bar — a statistically significant edge of at least 5 points over the hard null.
  • Strongest: Marshfield Wine & Spirits at +19.9 points.
  • Weakest: Mango Jo's Bar & Liquors at +6.1 points. Published rather than dropped — a store where the engine adds little is the most useful row here.
12 line item(s) across the estate were refused by the data contract before any figure was computed — single sale lines of more than 1,000 units, where the median line across these stores is 1 unit and the 99th percentile is 20.

Worth raising with whoever owns the till: one of them recorded 8,245,805 units of a 50ml Tito's at $0.00. Before the contract was applied it alone produced a reported incremental margin of $98.6M on a store turning over $7M a year. The danger is not that one — it is the same defect an order of magnitude smaller, which would look plausible.

How to reproduce every number here

cd backend
node scripts/export-store-sales.js all --out ../audit/data

cd ../Botella_External_APIs
python scripts/run_pilot_audit.py --data ../audit/data --out ../audit

The export is one SELECT against syncgo, which is read-only by design. Everything after it is a pure function of those files, so this whole audit replays months from now from the same inputs.

What none of this proves

This measures forecasting, not causation. Nobody acted on these recommendations — they were generated retrospectively. It shows the engine can point at products that were about to move. It does not show that following the advice causes extra sales. Only a live holdout, where some recommendations are shown and others deliberately withheld, proves that. The mechanism exists in production (REC_HOLDOUT_PCT) and needs switching on.
  • No live inventory. No historical shelf counts exist, so understock and overstock recommendations are absent from every figure here. The engine is judged on less than it normally sees.
  • The 7-day lead time is declared, never measured. Nothing in the warehouse records when an order actually arrived.
  • Marshfield has only 568 trading days, starting February 2025. It needs a year of history before a decision date, so it yields far fewer decision dates than the others and its interval is correspondingly weaker.

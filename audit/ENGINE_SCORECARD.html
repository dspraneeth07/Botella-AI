How to read a hit, in one sentence

A card is a hit when the product sold more over the window than its own trailing rate implied — a lift of 1.00× or better against the baseline the card itself published. It is not "the owner ordered it" and not "the product sold something": a product that was always going to sell is not credited to the engine.

02Holdout money — shown against withheld

The only scoreboard that answers "would it have sold anyway". A deterministic 10% of cards are withheld from the owner, computed and scored exactly like the rest, so the two arms can be compared.

This scoreboard cannot be computed yet, and these are the exact reasons

  • outcomes are SCORED for both arms — not true
  • some owner action is recorded — not true

The withheld arm has cards but no scored lift, so there is nothing to compare the shown arm against. And no owner action has been recorded anywhere: no votes, no action claims, and purchased_qty_source and delivery_evidence are null on every row — so "action inferred from stock jumps and invoices" is currently inferring nothing.

This is the scoreboard that answers "would it have sold anyway", and it is the one with no data. Prediction accuracy cannot stand in for it: a card can forecast perfectly and still change nothing, which is precisely what a holdout exists to detect.

The gates are the engine

Turning them off does not cost a few points — it inverts the result. The ungated ordering issues roughly twice as many cards, hits about half as often, and its margin per card goes from positive to deeply negative. The null column is the hard test: a volume- and reversion-matched random basket, so the edge is measured against products that were equally primed to recover on their own.

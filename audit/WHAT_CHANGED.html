2Why each number moved

1 — The ruler was wrong, so the old net margin was understated

The previous net margin charged the entire cost of goods for every unit a card said to buy that did not sell, as though the stock had been destroyed. Liquor does not spoil. A surplus bottle is deferred revenue, and its real cost is capital tied up, shelf space, and the chance it never sells at full price — cents a month on a $20 bottle, not $20.

That single line is the whole of “four of five stores are net negative”. It is corrected in app/pipeline/policy/holding.py, which charges capital plus shelf plus an obsolescence hazard by category family, and charges the hazard once rather than accruing it daily. The old figure is still published beside the new one as net_margin_cogs_ruler, so the size of the correction is visible rather than quietly absorbed.

This is a measurement correction. The engine's picks did not change, and nothing here should be read as the engine having improved.

2 — Non-merchandise left the measured path, and one store's score fell

$2 SCRATCHOFF and MARLBORO PACK are not restockable merchandise in the sense the engine models, and they were being scored. Screening them raised the pooled 30-day hit rate from 68.9% to 75.7%.

At Northlake it did the opposite: the lottery and cigarette lines were hitting 6-of-6 and 5-of-6 and inflating that store, so removing them took it from 54.7% down to 50.8%. The lower number is the truer one. A screen that only ever raises a score is a screen that is selecting on the outcome.

3 — A gate check could never pass, at any store, under any promise

promise_kept asked for 85–95% of cards to deliver at least the promised increment. One line of arithmetic retires it: a promise of at least one unit can only be kept on a card whose realised increment is at least one unit, so promise_kept ≤ hit rate — and the hit rates are 83 / 76 / 71 / 62 / 49%. It was the accuracy target restated as an independent test, and it could not pass until the thing it was meant to verify already had.

The field wired into it was a ratio of totals rather than a per-card rate, so the number being compared against the band was not even the same kind of quantity — Marshfield was handed a SHIP for under-delivering 11% in aggregate while Cork Runner failed for over-delivering. It is replaced by promise_unbiased, a band centred on 100%. The kept rate is still measured and printed, because the reason it is not gated has to stay checkable.

4 — Four of seven gate checks had never run

calibration_ece, no_unreliable_confidence, interval_coverage and both gate B coverage checks printed “not measured” at every store since the day the gate was written, because nothing computed the fields it reads. Verdicts were being issued on one live check out of four in gate A.

The reason interval_coverage could never run is worth stating plainly: the card published a point (“about 78 units”), so there was no range whose coverage could be checked. The card now publishes one, and it is the range the gate tests — measuring the coverage of an interval the owner never sees would be scoring the engine on a claim it never made.

The report also carried two rival definitions of the word ship: the index computed one from the backtest edge alone while the gate computed another from four different checks, for the same store, on the same page, and the looser one was in the headline. The edge test is now a check inside gate A (beats_null).

5 — The confidence can now be calibrated, and it is measured out of sample

The raw p_hit is a ranking score, not a probability, and the card says so in as many words. Measured against outcomes it is 34.1 / 31.4 / 23.9 / 12.0 / 3.5 points out at the five stores.

Fitting the isotonic curve on January and February and scoring March — so no store is graded on a slice it was fitted to — collapses that at three of five stores: Marshfield 34.1 → 0.3, Cork Runner 23.9 → 1.5, Bethany 12.0 → 2.6. At Northlake the curve makes it worse (3.5 → 15.0), because the raw score happened to be well calibrated there and the fit pulled it away. That is published too.

A fifth change made this measurable: a reliability bin holding five outcomes was condemning a whole store. At n = 30 and p = 0.5 the binomial standard error is 9.1 points, which is the floor at which a 10-point per-bin test stops measuring noise, so MIN_BIN_OUTCOMES = 30. Marshfield's real 171-card, 33.8-point failure still stands; Northlake's five-card one does not.

3The gate, check by check

Both gates must hold for a store to ship. A gate that does not apply does not block — a store sending no unit cost cannot be judged on money — but a check that was not measured is never a pass. Every threshold is a claim somebody can say is wrong, in which case it gets changed deliberately rather than drifting.

4What this still does not prove

The confidence curve does not reach production

The calibration checks above are measured honestly, but the fitted curve is not wired into the live card path. to_raw_recommendations still ships the raw p_hit. So those checks say the calibration method works on this store's data; they do not say an owner is seeing a calibrated percentage, because no owner is seeing a percentage at all. Closing it needs the fitted bundle persisted and loaded on the live route, which needs scored outcomes in recommendation_evaluations — and whether that table is populated in the deployed environment is still unverified.

  • Nobody acted on any of these recommendations. They were generated retrospectively from data the engine could legitimately have seen each morning, so this shows the engine points at products that go on to move. It does not show that acting on the advice causes the movement.
  • No shelf counts anywhere. Historical on-hand does not exist in the warehouse, so a dip caused by a stockout is indistinguishable from a dip caused by slow demand.
  • Lead times are declared, not measured. Nothing records when an order actually arrived.
  • Marshfield's high figures rest on few distinct products. Overlapping cards on the same SKU are treated as independent, so its ledger rates are cards-based and flatter a narrow catalogue.

The code is well tested and the engine is measurably better than random. The operation around it is not yet reliable.

Every automated suite that can run passes: 2,029 Python tests, three build guards, 14 offline Node suites, 15 of 17 database-backed Node suites, 79 frontend tests, a clean production build, and all 43 analytics migrations applied to an empty database. The two Node failures are a local-environment artefact and a six-day-old shelf capture, both explained below.

The engine's measured edge holds at all five pilot stores (+8 to +29 points over a null that already knows about mean reversion, p = 0.0005), but only 2 of 5 stores pass both release gates, the calibration curve is stale at all five, and the holdout experiment is too starved to state a causal difference anywhere.

Of 18 scheduled jobs, one date in the last eight (13 October) ran the full nightly chain. The laptop that hosts the local stack slept or rebooted through four of the other nights, one job (calibration refit) has failed on every run since it was written, one is a stub, and three are disabled. The forward test lost three of its first four mornings to the same cause. Fixes for the scheduling went in today; a reliability problem on a laptop is not a code problem, and the honest answer to "does it run every day" is in Section 07, job by job. By 16:30 the same day, defects 1, 3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15 and 16 were closed (commits 2da9658 to 01aa068), the Effectiveness tab was rebuilt so a card is "completed" only when the database shows the owner acted (f487427), the external-signal fit ran for the three deployment stores (b31e96f), and the scheduled generation completed for 67 stores including Bethany (8 cards) and Tuscany (10). Finding: the confidence figure, measured at Bethany (added 18:00)

Asked whether the stated confidence tells hits from misses. On the 129-card March/April ledger the AUC is 0.568 [0.470, 0.672] at 30 days: the interval straddles 0.5, and an out-of-sample isotonic fit made calibration worse (ECE 8.6 to 31.3 points) because it learned a March base rate April did not keep. On the live engine's forward test at Bethany, 202 resolved cards, rho is -0.315 and the AUC is 0.296 [0.200, 0.398], wholly below 0.5: higher stated confidence, lower hit rate, and the fitted curve collapses to one constant. Isotonic is monotone, so calibration cannot add the discrimination the figure lacks; p_hit is confined to 0.50-0.68 by its own form. Conclusion: the number is a ranking score, not a probability, and it is now hidden from owners, labelled on every card, and no longer used by the admission floor. What differentiates cards is expected margin, which the slate already orders on. Tables: audit/calibration/DIFFERENTIATION_2026-10-19.html.

Why the calibration refit fails

server.js passes --stores 2:2,4:4,… (client:location pairs) to publish_calibration.py, which parses each entry with int() and raises ValueError: invalid literal for int() with base 10: '2:2' at line 254. The job has never completed; the last published bundle (8 October) came from a manual run. This is a one-line interface mismatch and it is why the curve is stale at all five pilot stores.

The hosting problem, stated plainly

Every "when host is up" above is the same fact: the local stack runs on a laptop. It slept at 21:19 on the 16th and was off until 22:01 on the 17th; it rebooted three times around 02:30 on the 18th and neither service came back; it slept through 04:15 on the 19th. A deployed box (the GitLab deploy job exists and is manual) would make every row above "yes".

Mitigations added today on this host: the forward-test task fires four times a day plus at logon and is idempotent; a logon task restarts both services; the stack moved to ports 3010 / 8010 because another project now holds 3000. The tasks remain interactive-only (no admin rights) and so cannot run from a locked or freshly rebooted machine.

Finding: Tuscany's purchase dates (defect 17)

What the source holds. In syncgo, store 173's 7,646 purchase rows all carry -infinity in date (0 null, 0 sane) and a real invoice_date on every row (1 Dec 2019 to 17 Oct 2026; 254 invoices, 188 suppliers, 3,581 SKUs; 81 rows in the last 60 days). Stores 182, 184 and 186, the other LiquorPOS sites, show the identical pattern (3,808 / 6,188 / 1,786 rows). Every Korona, EPOSNow and PCAmerica store has a sane date; the PCAmerica stores whose query says null as InvoiceDate carry -infinity in invoice_date instead, the same mechanism in the other column. job_runs_history has no rows for 173, so the sync logged no warning.

What the sync does. The LiquorPOS extraction query is SELECT PO_NUM AS PurchaseId, DATE AS PurchaseDate, …, DATE AS InvoiceDate, … FROM ORD_IN WHERE QTY > 0. One Visual FoxPro column, ORD_IN.DATE, feeds both; the copy aliased InvoiceDate arrives intact, the copy aliased PurchaseDate does not, and every other connector's query names that column Date. The uploader binds by alias, finds no Date, writes its default DateTime, and the driver stores that as -infinity. The POS has the dates; the sync loses them on the alias. The fix belongs in SyncGo's config: DATE AS Date. It is not a VFP empty-date problem (an empty {} would read 1899-12-30, and there are none).

What this tree changed. Every reader of the purchase day now uses COALESCE(NULLIF(date,'-infinity'), NULLIF(invoice_date,'-infinity')), kept to days on or after 1990-01-01: the evaluator's two feed loaders, the PO-effectiveness feed count and per-SKU orders, StoreAgent's supplier cadence (median gap between orders), latest purchase cost and "what I bought" window, and the SQL-check mirrors. Lead times are untouched: an invoice day is an order day, not a receipt, and leadTimeService reads uploaded documents only. Pinned by tests/purchase-date-verify.js, scenario N of fulfilment-verify (a sentinel row through the mocked warehouse) and tests/test_supplier_repo.py.

Measured effect on 173. The evaluation re-ran for the real tenant: 30 cards, still 2 confirmed (both from stock inflow), 25 pending, 3 unobservable; no card is confirmed from the purchases feed. What changed is what the feed says: 12 rows across 12 SKUs and 5 invoices since 12 Oct where the old filter saw none, so the store is ordering, just not the 20 SKUs on its cards (4 of them have any history here; the last such purchase was 7 Feb 2026). The earlier sentence "no rows for any recommended SKU" was true on both readings; the reason it was true is now the store, not the column.

Finding: why Tuscany has 105 days of sales, and how to get three years (defect 21)

The DU uploader's first sales pull covers the 30 days before the first successful pull, so every LiquorPOS store onboarded on 4 Aug 2026 starts sales on 5 Jul; the purchases query has no date filter, which is why Tuscany's purchases reach back to 2019. INITIAL_PULL_FROM in client_pos_config.meta_data is empty for the LiquorPOS stores (it reads "2023-09-28" for 193 and "2023-10-01" for 214). Bethany went through the same thing and was re-pulled from 2023 on 31 Jul and 7 Aug, so the mechanism exists. The ask is in the go-live runbook (docs/GO-LIVE-RUNBOOK-THREE-STORES.md, section 6): set INITIAL_PULL_FROM = "2023-01-01" for 173, 184 and 186 and run a historical ranged sales pull 2023-01-01 to 2026-07-05, chunked by month. Rows dedupe on unique_hash, so overlap is safe. That gives Tuscany about 1,385 days of span, enough for the full backtest (492 days) and the 365-day reversion window.

On "world-class profitable"

Profitability is measured here, not promised. Bethany clears the $1,000-a-month profit gate ($1,356 in April, $24.65 per card) and fails the accuracy gate on a biased promise; Tuscany has no closed outcome window yet; Grapes and Grains has no data. The honest claim for the pilot is the one the gates make: a measured edge over mean reversion at every store that has data, money per card stated with its interval, and a refusal to state what has not been measured. The Effectiveness tab now reports owner follow-through from the database, which is what turns "profitable in a backtest" into "profitable in this shop" over the pilot months.
